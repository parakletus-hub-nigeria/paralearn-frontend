Question Stem (Section C - 20 Marks)

Explain the mathematical derivation of the quadratic formula from the standard form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) using the method of completing the square. Clearly state all intermediary algebraic constraints where \(a \neq 0\).

Standard Grading Key:
1. Divide through by a: x² + (b/a)x + c/a = 0
2. Transpose constant: x² + (b/a)x = -c/a
3. Add (b/2a)² to both sides: (x + b/2a)² = (b² - 4ac) / 4a²
4. Take square roots and isolate x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a.
Candidate Written Answer edit_note 184 words typed

To derive the quadratic formula from the general form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) when \(a \neq 0\):

First, divide the entire expression through by the leading coefficient \(a\), yielding:
x^2 + (b/a)x + c/a = 0.

Next, move the constant term to the right side of the equation: \(x^2 + (b/a)x = -c/a\).
Now we complete the square on the left side by taking half of the coefficient of \(x\), which is \(\frac{b}{2a}\), squaring it to get \(\frac{b^2}{4a^2}\), and adding this to both sides.

This simplifies the left side into a perfect square binomial: \((x + \frac{b}{2a})^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}\).
Taking the square root of both sides gives \(x + \frac{b}{2a} = \pm \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\). Subtracting \(\frac{b}{2a}\) yields the final formula: \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\).
